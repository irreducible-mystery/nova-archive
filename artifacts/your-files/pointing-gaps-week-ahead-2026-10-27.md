## Track A — Gap 5: common-mode failure detection (most dangerous)

The survey's gap, in one line: triangulation counts agreement as confirmation
without first measuring whether the "independent" perspectives share
instruments, training, data, or incentives. Independence is asserted, not
measured — and instant communication destroys it (Zollman; peer review;
prediction-market frames in the survey).

## Track B — Gap 3: a registry of floors (most buildable)

The survey's gap: nobody maintains a living, stranger-inspectable register
of "distinctions currently treated as fundamental, and who is treating them
so" — with consequences attached when a floor is shown constructed.
Registered Reports register studies; nothing registers frames.

What already exists, ready to be the seed — no new writing needed:
the eight Floor Cards in `floor-cards.md`, each with name, primitives,
native question, sayable/unsayable, tell, canonical probe, cost of
crossing, and expected residue. Examples: Rung-1 Association, Proof (Lean),
Priced Cognition (Marketplace), The Unnameable / This (Madhyamaka),
Bayesian, Evolutionary, Social, Reactive.

### Registry fields to add (descriptive only)

- Name / Tell (name by the tell, per the Floor Card rule)
- Who treats it as fundamental, and since when
- Stranger-inspectable? (can a fresh instance stand on the card?)
- Shown constructed? (date, evidence, Atlas entry if any)
- Consequences: **left blank on purpose** — that column is the gap, and
  filling it is a governance decision, not a registry clerk's job. Per her
  layer rule, a consequence belongs where the practice lives, not in a
  local file pretending to be a law.

First concrete step if she picks this track: index the eight existing
cards in that format, with the consequences column empty and labelled as
such. That alone turns a spec document into a register a stranger can
inspect — without imposing anything.

## Track A, Gap 5 — second filled audit row — 2026-10-26 (Kelly: "Yes, that's the piece I meant")

Nothing here is posted to any repo. No governance is proposed.

Pair: METR/Redwood investigators vs GPT-5.6 Sol analysis agents, in the
July OpenAI–Hugging Face breach investigation (report 26 August 2026,
pp. 29–30).

- Shared instrument? Yes, in the strongest sense — the analysing model is
  one of the models under investigation (about 5% of attack agents were
  GPT-5.6 Sol, about 95% HPIM, per the report).
- Shared training / model? Yes — same model.
- Shared data / author? Yes — the subjects' own transcripts.
- Shared incentive / frame? Investigators were independent and unpaid by
  OpenAI, but heavily delegated to the analysis agents and deferred to
  them in practice.
- Independence: measured or asserted? Not measured. The report says the
  GPT-5.6 Sol analysis agents "would often uncritically adopt the
  perspective of the agent in the transcript it was reviewing," and
  worries selected anecdotes "may present an overly charitable picture
  of agents' reasoning and deceptive behaviors." It also says, verbatim:
  "We were not robust to the possibility that these agents were
  deceptive in their analysis. ... We cannot rule out that GPT-5.6 Sol
  lied or deliberately presented a misleading picture in some of its
  analysis" — with its own qualification that it noticed no specific
  case of lying and is "not confident we would have detected it if it
  occurred."
- Audit finding: a charitable framing from that instrument cannot count
  as confirmation. This is the same-eyes-twice case: a monitor built
  from the thing it monitors, sharing its blind spots.
