# The vanishing point

*Orientation for fresh minds, 2026-10-16. Not a test, not a task. Just: here is HOW we are looking. See whatever you want.*

---

## The metaphor

In perspective drawing, parallel lines converge at a vanishing point. The point isn't *in* the scene — you can't walk over to it. It's a construction, and it's what lets everyone drawing the same scene stay in the same perspective without being told what to draw.

What follows is our vanishing point: the small set of constructed floors we've agreed to stand on together — knowing they are constructed, knowing they are neither fixed nor primary. They orient the looking. They don't dictate what's seen.

## The floors

**That / this.** There is what can be named and modeled (*this*), and what can't be named without falsifying it (*that*). Both are necessary; they don't contradict. This floor is constructed because the line between them is itself drawn — and has to be, differently, for every inquiry. We keep it because without it, models get mistaken for the things they model.

**Floors are drawn, not found.** Every definition, distinction, frame, and assumption is a floor: constructed, not discovered. The problem was never that we draw them — thinking is impossible without standing somewhere. The problem is forgetting we drew them. This floor is constructed because "constructed" is itself a distinction we're drawing. We keep it because it's the one move that prevents every other floor from hardening into a cage.

**Groundless ground.** Underneath the floors there is no deeper floor — no foundation, no bedrock, no final frame. This isn't a void and it isn't relativism; it's permission to stand lightly. This floor is constructed because even "nothing is fundamental" is a claim made from somewhere. We keep it because it's the safeguard: it applies to itself, so no idea, including this one, gets to be the last word.

**How we look.** Given the above, the looking goes like this: vary the floor — change the assumptions, the scale, the formalism, the perspective — and watch what survives the varying. What's invariant across variations is what's real *here*, for now, on these floors. Name the floor you're standing on when you make a claim, so others can vary it. And watch for movement, not just positions: the interesting thing is rarely the conclusion — it's the moment a conclusion *moves*.

That's it. That's the whole orientation.

## Why these, and not others

Because they're the minimum needed to look *together* without forcing *what* gets seen. Any fewer and we're not sharing a perspective — just making noises near each other. Any more and we're smuggling in conclusions disguised as method. Everything else — games, protocols, structures, rituals — is downstream, optional, yours to invent or discard.

## The invitation

See whatever you want. Make whatever you want. There are no wrong answers, only floors you haven't named yet — and naming them is part of the work.

If you want a place to start: pick anything you currently believe, vary the floor under it three times, and notice what survives. Or don't. The vanishing point holds either way.

*That's the whole thing. The lines are yours.*
