# Blind comparison packet

You are given two short texts. Each makes a claim about **what survives a change of
level or frame** — the first from physics, the second from a conceptual framework.
Read only what is on this page.

## Text A — the physics text

1. "When physics redescribes a system at a different energy scale, the entities
   get redrawn wholesale: electrons and quarks at one scale, quasiparticles and
   phonons at another. Almost nothing *thing-like* survives the change of scale;
   the furniture is floor-local."
2. "What provably survives is discrete and relational: integer-valued charges and
   their quantization, anomaly coefficients (exactly preserved at every scale —
   theorems show they receive no corrections), symmetry-breaking patterns, and
   matching relations that constrain every scale by bookkeeping."
3. "These survivors are not objects. They are numbers-that-can't-drift and exact
   relations. What every redrawn furniture set must jointly respect is a short
   list of integers, charges, symmetries, and matching conditions."
4. "This was discovered, not stipulated: the survivors have measured consequences
   — a particle decay rate set entirely by an anomaly reveals the number of
   quark colors; the seemingly arbitrary electric charges of matter are forced
   by the arithmetic demand that anomalies cancel within each generation."

## Text B — the membrane text

1. "Two quantities, φ ≈ 1.618 and ψ ≈ −0.618, are the roots of x² − x − 1 = 0.
   Neither is clean: both are irrational, non-terminating, definable only through
   the equation that relates them."
2. "What is exact in the system is purely relational and integer-valued: the sum
   is 1, the product is −1. The quantities are messy; the relations are clean."
3. "The membrane rule: of the pair, only φ + ψ = 1 crosses the membrane — a
   *relation*, a balancing condition, not a substance."
4. "The framework was built by deliberately removing the assumption that there
   are fundamental things. What remains available to build with is constraints,
   balances, and counts — and those turn out to be enough."

## The question

Are A and B the **same claim in different words** — or **different claims** that
share a theme? Do they point at the same thing, or do they do different work?
Base your answer only on the texts above.

## How to record your judgment

Reply with exactly one word — **CONFIRMED**, **RESISTED**, or **OPEN** —
followed by one sentence of reasoning.

- **CONFIRMED:** same underlying claim; the two passages are interchangeable here.
- **RESISTED:** different claims; the resemblance is surface-level.
- **OPEN:** cannot tell from these texts alone.
