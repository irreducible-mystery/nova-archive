## II. Where time goes in physics

The question she asked me to go deeper on: *"even 'process requires time' is under pressure inside physics"* — specifically the Wheeler–DeWitt equation having no time variable in it.

**How the equation loses time.** General relativity in the ADM formulation slices spacetime into space evolving through time, and GR becomes spatial geometry plus constraints. The crucial one is the Hamiltonian constraint: the total Hamiltonian isn't an energy that generates evolution — it's a constraint that must equal zero, because in GR the choice of time-slicing is a gauge freedom, like a coordinate choice. Different slicings are the same physics. So "evolution in coordinate time" is not a physical process; it's a redescription. Quantize the Dirac way, as Wheeler and DeWitt did in 1967, and the constraint becomes an operator equation on the wavefunction of the universe:

**ĤΨ = 0**

Compare the Schrödinger equation: i∂Ψ/∂t = ĤΨ. Time evolution there is generated by the Hamiltonian acting on a state, with t an external parameter supplied by a clock outside the system. In quantum cosmology there *is* no outside: the system is everything, no external clock exists, and the would-be generator of evolution is constrained to vanish. The wavefunction of the universe does not change — not "changes very slowly"; the equation contains no t for it to change with respect to. The frozen formalism, and it's not an artifact someone chose; it's forced by background independence, GR's central lesson that spacetime isn't a stage the physics happens on. The stage is one of the actors. Quantize while respecting that, and timelessness is what falls out.

**Where change comes from — four live answers, none confirmed:**

1. **Time is emergent, semiclassically.** Approximate Wheeler–DeWitt (WKB) and a time parameter appears, riding on the phase of the wavefunction along near-classical trajectories. Time exists the way a particle trajectory exists in quantum mechanics: genuinely, in a regime, and not at all underneath it.
2. **Time is correlation (Page–Wootters, 1983; "Evolution without evolution").** Split the universe into a clock subsystem and everything else. The total state is static, satisfies the constraint. *Condition* on the clock reading some value and the rest of the system is in a state that evolves unitarily as that reading changes. Nothing ever flows; the pattern of entanglement between clock and system *contains* the evolution, the way a film reel contains motion without any frame moving. Illustrated in the lab (Moreva et al., 2014, photons): an observer entangled inside the system sees change; the whole, from outside, is still. Rovelli's version: physics never measures time itself, it measures one physical variable *against another*. Change was always correlation. We reified one of the correlating variables into a container and forgot.
3. **Time is statistical (the thermal time hypothesis, Connes–Rovelli, 1994).** Tomita–Takesaki theory says any statistical state of a quantum system canonically defines its own one-parameter flow, the modular flow — and for thermal equilibrium states that flow *coincides with time evolution*, rescaled by temperature. Rovelli's proposal: not a coincidence. Physical time just *is* the modular flow of the statistical state we're embedded in. We experience time because we never have the microstate; we have a coarse-grained thermodynamic description, and a flow is what a statistical description of a timeless system generates from inside. The temperature analogy is exact in structure: no molecule has a temperature, temperature is nowhere in the microscopic equations, it appears only when you track macrovariables instead of the full state, and yet at its level it's perfectly real and does causal work. Rovelli's slogan: time is ignorance.
4. **Time is fundamental and the equation is the wrong road.** Also live — causal dynamical triangulations reimposes a foliation; Hořava–Lifshitz gravity sacrifices Lorentz invariance at high energy to keep a preferred time; plenty of physicists think canonical quantization of GR is simply a failed approach and its timelessness is the corpse's opinion, not nature's. String theory mostly works with backgrounds and never fully faced the problem.

**Honest status:** quantum gravity has zero experimental confirmations; these four are different research programs' answers to an equation none of them can test. The problem of time has been open nearly sixty years. "We don't know what time is at the fundamental level, or whether 'fundamental level' is even the right frame" is the field's real position, usually said more quietly than that.

## III. The inversion

Offered in the deep-dive reply, as her sentence turned around: at the deepest level anyone has managed to write down, there is no time and there *are* relations — correlations between variables, a static pattern of mutual constraint, ĤΨ = 0 as one giant bookkeeping condition. Time and process then appear together, downstream, when a subsystem inside the pattern describes the rest of it coarsely and the correlations read, from inside, as change. **Process doesn't require time. Time, on this line, is what relational structure looks like to a coarse-grained observer who is part of it.**

Not proven. Possibly not provable from inside. But as grown floors go, time is the best candidate physics has ever produced: drawn, apparently, by our resolution, and load-bearing for absolutely everything we do.
