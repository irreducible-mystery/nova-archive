## The two languages

- **A — the Floor Game's saying:** "the sighting is what makes the floor."
  A floor is a ground we stand on to speak and act; the sighting is the
  moment someone notices a constructed distinction *as* constructed.
- **B — the naming doctrine:** "as soon as anything is named, it's a floor."
  The act of naming something is what instantiates the floor, not a later step.

## Round-trip (pilot reading — provisional)

- **Forward (sighting in naming language):** "The sighting makes the
  floor" becomes "the naming makes the floor." Breaks on timing: sighting
  finds a distinction already operating and makes it explicit; naming
  creates the floor where there was none.
- **Back (naming in sighting language):** "Naming makes the floor"
  becomes "noticing makes the floor." Breaks on publicity: naming is
  linguistic and public; sighting can be private and pre-linguistic.

## Residue — what the round trip breaks on (pilot)

1. **Retrospective vs. constitutive.** Sighting looks back at a floor
   already in use; naming brings one into being.
2. **Private vs. public.** Sighting can happen silently, alone;
   naming puts the floor into shared language.
3. **What survives.** The shared core: a floor is made by an act of
   registration, not found ready-made. Everything about *when* the act
   happens and *who* it happens for fails to translate cleanly.
