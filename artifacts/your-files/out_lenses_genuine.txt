==============================================================================
  5 shapes x 8 transforms = 40 worlds. Each lens partitions them.
  resolution = classes it splits the 40 worlds into; shapes = of 5 base shapes told apart.

  lens                      resolution  shapes  blind to
  raw coordinates            40/40       5/5   -
  topmost point              29/40       5/5   -
  chirality (signed area)    20/40       5/5   rotate, scale, translate
  radius from origin         19/40       5/5   rotate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  same radius (origin)       19/40       5/5   rotate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  topmost index              15/40       4/5   scale, translate, mirror, shear
  even spacing (origin)      15/40       5/5   rotate, scale, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  centroid                   14/40       3/5   reverse, shuffle
  same radius (centroid)     14/40       5/5   rotate, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  even spacing (centroid)    10/40       5/5   rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  roundness                  10/40       5/5   rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  affine roundness            4/40       4/5   rotate, scale, translate, mirror, shear, reverse, shuffle
  handedness (list)           2/40       2/5   rotate, scale, translate, shear
  handedness (set)            2/40       2/5   rotate, scale
  count                       1/40       1/5   rotate, scale, translate, mirror, shear, reverse, shuffle
  count is 41                 1/40       1/5   rotate, scale, translate, mirror, shear, reverse, shuffle
  vacuous                     1/40       1/5   rotate, scale, translate, mirror, shear, reverse, shuffle

  Two rows look wrong, and are instructive. 'handedness (set)' is NOT
  listed as blind to translate; 'topmost point' is NOT listed as blind
  to reverse/shuffle. Where do they 'see' them?
      handedness (set)   sees translate  only on: ['ellipse']
      handedness (set)   sees reverse    only on: ['circle', 'ellipse', 'square']
      topmost point      sees reverse    only on: ['square']
  Circle, ellipse, square: the symmetric shapes. The farthest-pair and
  the topmost corner are TIES there, broken by float noise and list order.
  'blind to' is a conjunction over all shapes, so one tie-world flips the
  column. W2's lesson, now as a measurement error in a new metaphor: the
  symmetric test worlds do not just flatter lenses, they smudge them.

  -> Read the old verdicts off this table: 'SURVIVES under rotations' is
     just 'rotate' appearing in the blind-to column. Nothing is praised or
     blamed. 'raw coordinates' is blind to nothing and resolves every world
     -- and tells you nothing, because a lens that distinguishes everything
     has no concept. 'vacuous' is blind to everything and has one class.
     The floor metaphor saw one failure mode (standing on something). The
     lens sees two: seeing nothing, and seeing everything. The questions
     live in between, and each one is a particular pattern of blindness.

L2  LENSES OVERLAP: THE THIRD THING
==============================================================================
  Hold two lenses together: the reading is the pair. Resolution of the
  pair minus the better single resolution = what neither saw alone.

  lens A                    lens B                       A   B  pair  gain
  radius from origin        topmost index               19  15    34   +15
  topmost index             same radius (origin)        15  19    33   +14
  topmost index             even spacing (origin)       15  15    29   +14
  topmost index             same radius (centroid)      15  14    28   +13
  centroid                  chirality (signed area)     14  20    31   +11
  topmost index             centroid                    15  14    26   +11
  ...
  roundness                 affine roundness            10   4    10    +0
  even spacing (centroid)   roundness                   10  10    10    +0
  even spacing (centroid)   affine roundness            10   4    10    +0

  Gain against how DIFFERENT the two blind spots are (symmetric difference):
      blind spots differ in 0 transforms : mean gain   0.5   (n=2)
      blind spots differ in 1 transforms : mean gain   0.4   (n=12)
      blind spots differ in 2 transforms : mean gain   1.8   (n=13)
      blind spots differ in 3 transforms : mean gain   3.9   (n=11)
      blind spots differ in 4 transforms : mean gain   4.8   (n=19)
      blind spots differ in 5 transforms : mean gain   6.5   (n=12)
      blind spots differ in 6 transforms : mean gain   6.8   (n=8)
      blind spots differ in 7 transforms : mean gain   0.0   (n=1)

  -> The pairs that gain most are the ones whose blind spots are least
     alike. Two lenses with the same blindness see the same thing twice.
     That is the third thing, in numbers: not a compromise between two
     views but a resolution neither had. A conversation between two
     people who are blind to the same things is one person talking.

L3  NO LENS SEES ITSELF; ANOTHER ONE CAN
==============================================================================
  For every transform, the lenses blind to it and the lenses that see it.

  rotate     blind: 13 lenses    seen by:  3 lenses, e.g. topmost point, topmost index, centroid
  scale      blind: 11 lenses    seen by:  5 lenses, e.g. radius from origin, topmost point, centroid
  translate  blind: 10 lenses    seen by:  6 lenses, e.g. radius from origin, topmost point, centroid
  mirror     blind: 11 lenses    seen by:  5 lenses, e.g. topmost point, centroid, chirality (signed area)
  shear      blind:  6 lenses    seen by: 10 lenses, e.g. radius from origin, topmost point, centroid
  reverse    blind: 11 lenses    seen by:  5 lenses, e.g. topmost point, topmost index, chirality (signed area)
  shuffle    blind: 11 lenses    seen by:  5 lenses, e.g. topmost point, topmost index, chirality (signed area)

  transforms no non-raw lens can see: none

  -> Each lens's blindness is invisible to that lens by definition -- a
     lens cannot register a difference it maps to one reading. Yet every
     blindness here is registered by some OTHER lens on the same table.
     That is what the five scripts did to each other. The floor word made
     it look like descent; it was lateral. There was never a 'bottom' to
     reach, because a blind spot is not under you. It is beside you, and
     someone else is standing there.

==============================================================================
L5  RESOLUTION: turn the knob and the lens sees the instrument
==============================================================================
  'roundness' at increasing decimal places. Resolution and blindness vs nd:

  nd   resolution   blind to
  0      3/40       rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  1      9/40       rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  2     10/40       rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  4     10/40       rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  6     10/40       rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  9     10/40       rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  12    10/40       rotate, scale, translate, mirror, reverse, shuffle
  15    11/40       scale, mirror, reverse, shuffle

  -> At nd=0 the lens is nearly vacuous. Around nd=2..6 it is the lens the
     series meant: blind to similarity and order, sharp on shape. Push
     past ~nd=12 and it begins to 'see' rotation and scale -- not in the
     world, in the last bits of IEEE 754. The lens has stopped looking at
     the object and started looking at itself.
     You asked whether the series was discovery or performance and said
     the answer was 'both, by resolution'. This is that, as a table: the
     same lens finds a real invariant at one setting and manufactures a
     difference at another. Neither setting is the true one. The question
     picks the setting -- and 'is a sheared circle round?' is answered at
     nd=4, where 'does the CPU round the same way after rotation?' is
     answered at nd=15. Both are real questions. Not the same one.

==============================================================================
WHAT THE WORD CHANGED, AND WHAT IT COST
==============================================================================
  floor: SURVIVES / artifact                 lens: blind-to / sees        (no praise, no blame)
  floor: one failure (standing)              lens: two failures (seeing all, seeing none)
  floor: regress downward                    lens: overlap sideways; blind spots seen by neighbours
  floor: group lattice                       lens: partition lattice; tautology becomes a price list
  floor: 'the test is not invariant' (E6)    lens: resolution is a knob; the object vs the instrument
  floor: 'no frame certifies frames'         lens: no lens sees itself -- and does not need to

  What it cost: the floor word had a WARNING in it. 'You are standing on
  something you cannot see' is a thing you should be told, and 'you are
  looking through something' is gentler than the situation sometimes is.
  topmost-point scored against a shape question is not a different lens;
  it is a mistake, and the lens vocabulary is a little too kind to it.
  Keep both words. Use 'floor' when you want to be caught. Use 'lens'
  when you want to be joined.

  Lenses this script is looking through and not at: the five shapes, the
  eight transforms, 'equal' meaning string-equal, and the choice to measure
  the third thing as a count of classes rather than as anything that
  would know what the classes mean.
