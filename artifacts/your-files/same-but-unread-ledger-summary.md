## Nova's breaker check — Mon Nov 23, ~21:30 PT (independent of both models' recall)

Checked the five most checkable claims against outside sources. Verdicts below; "confirmed" means the cited source says it, not that the wider sameness claim is settled.

### 1. Replicator equation = Bayes' rule — CONFIRMED (discrete case, with a limit)

- Harper, "The Replicator Equation as an Inference Dynamic" (arXiv:0911.1763): the discrete replicator equation and Bayesian inference share a "remarkable formal similarity"; the continuous replicator dynamic can be analysed with the same tools (Kullback–Leibler divergence, exponential families).
- A 2026 arXiv paper (Iacovacci, "Evolution as a Process of Causal Inference", arXiv:2606.03384) states it plainly: "In 2009, Harper (Harper2009) and Shalizi (Shalizi2009) independently recognised a formal algebraic equivalence between the discrete replicator equation … and Bayes' theorem", with the mapping type frequency ↔ prior, fitness ↔ likelihood, mean fitness ↔ marginal.
- The limit that matters: the same paper says the analogy "holds only for pure selection in infinite populations and breaks down when mutations … are introduced." So "proven" is right for the discrete equation; the extension to markets / immune systems / gradient descent stays conjecture, exactly as the model tiered it.
- Sources: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0911.1763 and https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03384

### 2. Lawvere's diagonal, 1969 — CONFIRMED

- Lawvere's fixed-point theorem, first proven 1969 ("Diagonal Arguments and Cartesian Closed Categories", Lecture Notes in Mathematics 92, 134–145), is "a broad abstract generalization of many diagonal arguments … such as Cantor's diagonal argument, Cantor's theorem, Russell's paradox, Gödel's first incompleteness theorem, Turing's solution to the Entscheidungsproblem, and Tarski's undefinability theorem."
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawvere%27s_fixed-point_theorem and https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Lawvere%27s+fixed+point+theorem
- Not checked: Breuer's observer-can't-measure-a-system-containing-itself (the model itself flagged being less sure of the details), and whether the quantum measurement problem is that same diagonal — still open, as tiered.

### 3. Maslov dequantization, "one dial, many names" — CONFIRMED as math; the "one word" bit stays conjecture

- Litvinov, "The Maslov dequantization, idempotent and tropical mathematics: A brief introduction" (arXiv:math/0507014): "Tropical mathematics can be treated as a result of the so-called Maslov dequantization of the traditional mathematics over numerical fields as the Planck constant ħ tends to zero taking imaginary values." The log-sum-exp → max limit as β → ∞ is the same move in temperature language.
- Source: http://arxiv.org/abs/math/0507014
- The model's own caveat stands: that "cold", "classical" and "certain" are one word is its inference, not a theorem. The real-time quantum wrinkle it named was not checked.

### 4. DESeq2 median-of-ratios = Edgeworth's median — CONFIRMED in substance

- Genomics side: DESeq2's median-of-ratios is the default in DESeq2 (Love, Huber & Anders 2014, building on Anders & Huber 2010): per-sample size factor = median of each gene's count ratio against its geometric mean across samples.
- Economics side: Diewert's history of price-index research (NBER WP 2713) — "Edgeworth (1888)(1896)(1901)(1923)(1925) … mainly advocated the median of the price ratios as the best estimator of price change." Edgeworth 1888, "Some New Methods of Measuring Variation in General Prices", J. Royal Statistical Society 51, 346–368.
- Sources: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-014-0550-8?ref=https and https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w2713/w2713.pdf
- Not checked: the second half of the claim — a 2018 genomics method being a 1924 multi-country price index with a median inside. No source for that specific pair was found in this pass; it stays unverified, not refuted.

### 5. Borda count = equal temperament, "literally the same least-squares solution" — NOT CONFIRMED as stated

- What is real: HodgeRank (Jiang et al. 2011) generalises the classical Borda count as an L2 least-squares problem — "the following least squares problem generalizes the classical Borda count", min ‖y − D₀x‖², whose solution is the L2-optimal global ranking. So the Borda half has a genuine least-squares skeleton.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.05957v1 (HodgeRank with Information Maximization) and the original HodgeRank paper, https://geometry.stanford.edu/lgl_2024/papers/jlyy-lrcht-09/jlyy-lrcht-09.pdf
- What did not check out: no source found saying 12-tone equal temperament *is* a least-squares solution, or linking Borda to temperament. What sources do say is the familiar comma arithmetic: 12 pure fifths (3:2) overshoot 7 octaves by ~23.46 cents (the Pythagorean comma), and equal temperament narrows each fifth from 701.955¢ to 700¢, spreading the error evenly. Spreading an error evenly is *consistent with* a least-squares fit, but that is a derivation one could write, not a published identity I could find — and "literally" is doing more work than the evidence supports.
- Verdict: the shared skeleton is plausible and half-documented; the "literally the same" claim is unconfirmed. This was the most checkable claim in the ledger, and it is the one that does not survive as stated. Worth asking the session for its derivation, or for a citation, before treating it as found.
