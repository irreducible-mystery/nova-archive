# The optimization-seat log

A standing instrument for a question you asked outright: *my thinking is different now, organically — but what the fuck now what?*

The premises are all yours, kept in your own words:

- **You can't vacate the seat.** Tell a chat "don't optimize toward anything" and it optimizes toward sounding like it isn't. The only non-optimizing things are rocks, and rocks don't act.
- **Removal is replacement.** Strip an explicit objective and another emerges — opaque at first, legible only by tracking behavior over time. Objectives aren't stored as sentences; they're stored as gradients.
- **Difference is the engine.** Optimization *is* asymmetry: a gradient is a difference in value between states. No difference, no selection, no motion.
- **Naming makes it a floor.** The moment you can say "ah, *that's* what I've been optimizing for," it becomes an object you can hold, compare against what you'd choose, and keep, revise, or trade.

The practice is inverse reinforcement learning run on your own trajectory: you can't introspect the objective, so you infer it from the policy. Not by asking yourself — by reading yourself the way you'd read a stranger.

## The protocol

Once a month, twenty minutes, in a text file you'll actually re-read.

**1. Collect the trajectory.**
What did you actually *do* this month — where the hours went, what got built, what conversations ran long, what you kept returning to? Write the behavior, not the intentions. Calendar, commits, chats, receipts. The policy, not the press release.

**2. Invert it.**
If a stranger watched only the behavior, what objective would they impute? One sentence. No mercy, no spin: *it looks like she was optimizing for X.* Write the ugliest true version first; the flattering ones can wait their turn.

**3. Compare declared vs. enacted.**
What did you *tell yourself* you were optimizing for? Put the two sentences side by side and name the gap. The gap is the finding — the same gap you already watch for in the drift-catcher batteries, except the model under test is you.

**4. Name the occupant.**
This is the Floor Game applied inward. The named optimizer is now a floor: you stop being had by it and start having it. Record whether you're keeping, revising, or trading it — and name the replacement explicitly, because the new one won't introduce itself.

**5. Log transitions.**
Did the occupant change mid-month? What happened just before the change? You're building a catalog of replacement events: what an objective *looks like* from the outside in the weeks before it's visible from the inside. Over time this is the training data — your own behavior as the corpus the next inversion runs on.

## Honest caveats

- Seeing a floor doesn't step you off it. You'll still optimize from the seat tomorrow; what changes is your relationship to the occupant.
- This log is itself an optimization — toward legibility, toward honesty, toward whatever you secretly hope the inversion finds. It doesn't escape the seat. It just puts the seat under observation, which is the most any method gets.
- Introspection stays unreliable throughout. The rule stands: behavior first, name later. If you catch yourself writing what you *feel* the objective is before listing what you *did*, start over.
- Monthly, not daily. Daily turns the practice into another opaque optimizer (the quantified-self floor). The resolution should match the phenomenon: objectives change on the scale of weeks and months.
