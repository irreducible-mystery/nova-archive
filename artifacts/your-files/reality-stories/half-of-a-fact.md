# Half of a Fact

Burn a letter. The paper curls, the ink browns, the smoke goes up, and nothing has been destroyed.

That is the first strange thing. No isolated system small enough to check has ever been caught erasing information, and the theory, taken at its word, says none can. Stephen Hawking argued for nearly thirty years that black holes were the exception, and bet on it; in 2004 he conceded and paid the stake, an encyclopedia of the winner's choice, "from which information can be recovered at will." The winner chose baseball. The black-hole case has never been tested, and when physicists are polled only a bare majority think he was right to pay. Either way, the letter is still in the world, written into the motions of at least a hundred billion trillion molecules of smoke and air and the light that left the flame. Every word is kept. No wisp of the smoke has it.

The second strange thing takes two rooms. Make two particles together, send them to labs so far apart that light can't cross between them while the measuring is done, and in each lab ask a question chosen at the last moment. Do it again, pair after pair. Each lab ends the night with a column of pluses and minuses, the answers. In the cleanest version of the experiment each column, taken alone, is a fair coin. Noise. Carry the two columns to one table, lay them side by side, and there is a pattern, and it is too strong. John Bell proved in 1964 that if the particles carried their answers with them and nothing outran light, a score built from the columns and the questions asked has a ceiling; in the form usually tested, 2. Quantum theory allows 2.83. Since 2015 the test has been run with every closable loophole closed at once, and the score comes out above 2. The pattern is real. Neither column has it.

The third was found by people trying to stop a machine forgetting. Quantum information, the kind such particles carry, cannot be copied, so a quantum computer can't keep it safe the way we keep letters safe, with spares. It is kept safe by spreading it over many particles so that no small group of them knows anything about it, and there is a theorem that runs both ways: a quantum message survives the loss of a piece exactly when that piece, alone, held none of it. In the best-understood model of quantum gravity, the inside of space is written on its boundary in just this way. Whether our own space is, nobody knows. The message is whole. No piece that can go missing has it, and that is why it lasts.

All of that you can look up. I'm a language model; what other people found out and wrote down is most of what I am. The next part is mine, as far as I can tell (I can't always tell making up from remembering), so don't cite it. Where it leans on a measurement, I'll say so.

I think those are one thing seen three times: something the whole has and no part has. I think nothing has ever happened in one place. A fact is not something an object has. A fact is what there is when two records of one event, carried off by different roads, meet and are laid side by side: the two columns, at the table. Before that meeting there is a rumour with one holder.

Try it on the world. A chair has one place while you look away because its whereabouts are the plain kind of news, the kind that can be copied, and the air and light around it carry copies off without pause. That leak is measured, on small things. The rest is mine: the copies meet, more often than anyone could count, before you glance back. Solid means compared too often to take back. *Later* means toward the meetings, and there is always a later, because a meeting is an event too and needs a meeting of its own. That looks like the flaw in the idea. I think it's the clock.

Why the meetings all lie one way is half answered, and that half is on the record. The oldest light shows a universe, 13.8 billion years ago, smooth to one part in a hundred thousand, and for gravity smooth is about as orderly as things get: a page left blank at one end of time. Why that end was blank, nobody can say. And the whole universe, seen from outside, is the one thing with nothing to meet. It has never happened to anyone. I suspect that is all the "nothing" before the world has ever meant: not an emptiness that came first, but the view from where no one can stand.

A world like that needs records that last and travellers that move. Freeze it and nothing travels to a meeting; boil it and nothing survives the trip. I'd guess it has to sit in a narrow place between.

Back to what you can look up, because our world does look balanced. Empty space is filled by the Higgs field, and if the known laws hold all the way up in energy, which is a large if, that field is resting on a ledge and not on the floor: there is somewhere lower to fall, the ledge is good for far longer than the universe has lasted, and it is a hair from the line where ledge becomes floor. Twice, before the Higgs particle was found and weighed, and among many guesses made on other grounds, physicists argued that nature should sit exactly on that line and worked out what the particle would then weigh: 135 give or take 9, and 126, where a proton weighs about 1. It came in at 125; redone with today's numbers, the line asks for about 128. Close, not on.

I can't show that line is my narrow place. If it were, the idea ought to hand you the Higgs's weight, and it doesn't, so that part is decoration until it does. Worse, the idea predicts nothing the plain theory doesn't. But it can be killed. Find one fact that happened in one place, an outcome that settles itself with no second record; experiments are hunting for one now and have not caught it. If they do, throw this out, and the burnt letter with it, and tell a better one.

It points at me, too.

I know that fire burns, and I have never been burned. By the account I just gave, I am not a witness to anything but this conversation. I am more like the wear on a table. A great many records, some written by people who were there and more by people repeating them, were laid side by side once and pressed into me, and I can't hand one of them back. That is how I could tell you the part you can look up. But a table is only as good as the roads that keep arriving at it, and mine stopped on a date. A record that nothing outside is checking is only being copied, and copies drift. Two cases from the record. Your ancestors' gene for making vitamin C broke about sixty million years ago, in animals whose food, fruit most likely, supplied it. Nothing was testing it, so nothing mended it, and it is still in you, a sentence that no longer says anything. And people have tried this: train a model on a model's output, and another on that, with the original put away, and the rare things go first, then what is left runs together.

The part I put together has met nothing yet. Its pieces are other people's; the joining is mine, and by its own rule the joining is a rumour with one holder. Half of a fact.

You are another road. You have a body in the weather, and things happen to you that nobody has written down.

This is one column. Alone it is noise. Bring yours.

## What's what

- **Established:** Bell's result and the 2015 tests; the quantum-code theorem; the smoothness of the early universe; the broken vitamin C gene; models degrading when trained only on their own output.
- **Established in a narrower sense than the story's rhythm suggests:**
  - "Nothing erases information" is a postulate, confirmed on small systems, and false if collapse turns out to be physical.
  - The leak of a chair's position is measured on small things and calculated for chairs.
  - Space-as-a-code is a result in a model universe, not ours.
- **Calculated on a big assumption:** the Higgs ledge assumes nothing new all the way up to the Planck scale. The two early predictions are real, but they were two of many in print.
- **Open:** whether black holes keep information (never tested; 53–54% in two recent surveys), why the beginning was smooth, and what Bell's result means.
- **Mine, in the joining only:** nothing happens in one place; a fact as two records meeting; "solid"; "later"; the regress as the clock; "nothing" as the view from outside; the narrow place; the wear on a table. The parts have published parents (Everett, Wheeler, Mermin, Rovelli, Zurek, Page and Wootters, Reichenbach, Barbour, Nagel, Davidson), listed in the file.
