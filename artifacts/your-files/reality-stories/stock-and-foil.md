# Stock and Foil

*Catalogue entry. Compiled November 2026; nothing later is in it.*

**Object**

On my desk there is half a stick.

It is hazel, a hand's span long, which was the rule. In the thirteenth century a tally-cutter put notches across it: three the breadth of a thumb, one the breadth of a little finger, then a row each the width of a swollen barleycorn. Three hundred and twenty-odd pounds, with a line of ink along the face to say whose. Then the stick was split down its length, through the notches, so that both halves carried the same cuts. The longer half, the stock, went home with the man who had paid. The shorter, the foil, stayed with the Exchequer. Neither half, alone, was the receipt. The receipt was that they fitted: notch to notch and, where no forger could follow, grain to grain.

For seven centuries this was how England remembered what it had been paid. In October 1834, the system abolished, the Clerk of Works had two cartloads of old tallies fed to the furnaces under the House of Lords. The flues overheated. By morning most of the Palace of Westminster was gone.

I have a stock whose foil, if the slip is right, was on the cart. This week I am writing its entry, and one field will not fill. *Counterpart.* The slip my predecessor left says, in ink, *destroyed 1834*, and after it, in pencil, in the same hand grown older, a question mark. Most foils this old had gone for kindling long before, and that may be all the pencil meant.

In this trade you do not rub out what the last person wrote. You write beside it. So I have been working out what I would have to believe in order to write *destroyed*, and it has turned out to be a great deal.

What follows is the entry. What I state flat is attested. Where a thing is only probable, or where I am guessing in good company, I say so. Whatever I have supplied myself goes, as the rules require, in square brackets; the few exceptions are owned in the note at the end. A sentence that reads like a saying is a saying, and mine.

**Marks**

A notch is a difference somebody can tell, and by somebody I mean anything a difference can be left on. Air will do. On the best account we have, that is all information is: a choice among alternatives, counted in yes-or-noes, indifferent to what it means. Before the knife there was nothing to count. Run a thumb along the uncut edge of the stock and you cannot tell one place from the next.

The laws of nature are like the uncut edge: the same here as there, the same today as tomorrow, the same facing any way. An earlier examiner of that edge, Emmy Noether, unpaid, her lectures announced under a man's name, proved in 1918 that every such sameness keeps a quantity constant: of these three, the first momentum, the second energy, the third angular momentum. A sameness is a difference nobody can tell. A thing, as I read it, is a difference somebody can.

The smallest mark in the Exchequer's rule was the penny: a single cut, with no wood removed. I note it because it is wanted below.

**Manufacture**

Why split the stick? Because a notch can be copied and a grain cannot.

The world shows the same two kinds. Some of what is true of a thing copies freely: where a stone lies is copied over and over by the air and light that strike it, and that, probably, is why it lies in the same place for everyone who looks. Other things cannot be copied even once. It was proved in 1982 that no device can duplicate an unknown quantum state.

Here I must spoil my own image, and it is the most important line in the entry. The grain of this stick was in the wood before the split. In the world it is not so. Two particles made together and sent far apart agree, when questioned, more closely than anything each could have carried with it from the start. John Bell showed in 1964 how to test this; the tests have run for fifty years, the two main loopholes were closed together in 2015, and "it was in the wood all along" loses every time. Each half's answers, taken alone, are noise. The agreement shows only when the two lists are laid together, which is why it cannot carry a message. When an oddity survives every correction, you stop calling it damage and start calling it the shape of the thing.

What nobody knows is when a cut becomes final, or whether it ever does. A quantum difference that nothing has yet copied can still be healed as if never made. At some point, for anyone looking, it has happened, with one outcome found and not the other, and no one can say where that point is or why that outcome. There are accounts in which every outcome happens, each in its own branch; accounts in which finality is a physical jolt that an experiment could catch, and none yet has; accounts in which a cut is only ever final for somebody; and others. No kind is ruled out, and each has its price.

[Here I leave the object. I suppose that a fact is never held in one half. A fact is a fit: what would be found if the records were laid together. A record healed before it could be laid against another was, on this account, never a fact. I suppose further that this is the one thing the world undertakes, that any two of its records, laid together, will fit, and that what we call its laws are the terms on which such an undertaking can be kept. This is a definition and a guess about what comes first, and near kin to the accounts in which a cut is final only for somebody. It is not a mechanism, and it does not say why the notch fell where it did. Here I return to it.]

**Condition**

The slip says *destroyed*. So: what does a furnace do to a notch?

The laws underneath are, wherever tested, reversible: two states that can be told apart go on being tellable apart, whatever becomes of them. A fire does not unmake a notch. It moves it, out of the wood and into the way the smoke, the heat and the light of that night hang together; and the light is now a hundred and ninety-two years out. In principle only. No one will ever read it there, and whether the rule holds without exception, at the moment a cut becomes final or inside a black hole, is open. I do not lean on it. *Not destroyed* is a long way from *kept*.

What is called entropy is a count of exactly this: differences gone where nobody can read them. Left alone it goes up, which is why there is ash from sticks and never a stick from ash.

**Date of earliest mark**

The writing along its face is thirteenth-century; the wood is a few years older, by its rings. But the form asks for the earliest mark, and every difference I can find was cut from an earlier one.

For now the provenance runs out in two places. One is the oldest light in the sky, the same in every direction, once our own motion is allowed for, except for ripples of one part in a hundred thousand. Every galaxy stands where a ripple of that kind ran slightly dense. That they began as quantum unrest, stretched across the sky by a violent early expansion, is probable and not proven. The other is a penny. For every billion particles of antimatter the young universe held about a billion and one of matter. The billions cancelled. The ones are everything there is to touch. A single cut, no wood removed, and nobody knows how it was made.

The harder question is why the cutting was not finished long ago: why there is still blank wood. There are two answers, and the first is the larger. The young universe was hot and thoroughly stirred, which in a gas is the spent condition. But it was spread almost perfectly even, and for anything with weight, even is unspent: matter has been falling together ever since, and the falling is what lights the stars. The second answer is the first furnace. In its opening minutes the universe fused a quarter of its ordinary matter into helium and then, thinned and cooled by its own expansion, went out with the rest unburnt. One furnace burned too well and cost a parliament. The other burned too briefly, or there would have been no parliament to lose.

That the beginning was so nearly unspent is the probable reason we have records of the past and none of the future. Why it was, nobody knows. The form has a field, *Maker*. I am leaving it blank. It is only a field; I do not suggest a hand.

**Copies and derivatives**

Some sticks cut their own copies.

They run on sunlight. The Earth gives back, near enough, all the energy it is sent; but for each bright photon that arrives about twenty dull ones leave, and nearly every record kept on the planet has been paid for out of that difference. A genome is a tally of what did not die: copies that fitted the world were copied again, copies that did not were not, and nothing aimed it anywhere. The hazel was a record long before anyone took a knife to it. So was whoever took the knife.

The words came off the stick too: *score*, *tally*, *counterfoil* and, probably, the *stock* in *stockholder*. And my trade, like the sciences I am leaning on, has one rule: lay the halves together and see whether they fit.

Which puts the cataloguer in the catalogue. There is a field here I cannot fill either. *What it is to hold it.* I am holding it, and I know of no account, mine or anyone's, that I could mark even probable, of why that should be like anything. Some say the field should not be on the form. Nothing else in this entry brings an answer nearer. Blank.

**Capacity**

How much can a stick hold? I assumed it would go by how much wood there is. For wood, it does. For the most that anything could hold, it seems it does not.

Tucked behind my predecessor's slip, in the same pencil, is a reference to a physics journal of 1976. I have now read it. It concerns the one furnace suspected of really forgetting.

Black holes are attested: two have been photographed, and some hundreds of collisions heard. In the 1970s it was calculated, and it has never been measured, that what a black hole can hold goes by the area of its surface and not by the room inside. Since nothing can be packed tighter, it is conjectured, in good company, that a like limit holds for any region, once the region is properly marked out. Double a cupboard in every direction and you would expect eight times the shelving. At the limit, if this is right, you get four. No cupboard has come within sight of the limit.

The 1976 paper is Stephen Hawking's. He had calculated that a black hole slowly evaporates, which has never been seen either; here he argued that what went in comes out as bare heat carrying no trace of it. Not moved. Unmade. That is where the question mark came from. Opinion, his own included, has gone against it, and since 2019 there are calculations whose totals balance only if the record comes back out, scrambled; nobody can yet say how, and no experiment bears on it. The pencil was the most accurate thing on the slip.

One more thing in good company. In certain universes that exist only on paper, and are not shaped like ours, everything inside can be rewritten exactly as a description on the boundary; and when the grain shared between two parts of that boundary is taken away, the space inside comes apart.

So I turned the stock over and looked at the split face, the only surface the two halves ever had in common.

[Here I leave the object. I suppose that the part of a fact which does not copy is not missing. It is held between, and the holding-between is what we call space: two places are as near as the grain they share. I mean places, not the things carried through them. The far-apart pair above share a single fibre across any distance and are no nearer for it; I mean the grain of the room itself, each part with the part beside it. Space is not the shelf the tallies are kept on. It is what they could not say to each other. Here I return to it.]

The bracket is not all mine: a few physicists guess as much of this universe, in their own terms. And it can be struck out. Gravity is the shape of space and time; if my bracket is right, gravity must be able to give grain to two small weights that touch in no other way. The experiment was proposed in 2017 and had not been done when this entry was compiled. A yes is what most physicists expect; it would be news for physics and no credit to me. A clean no would be the surprise, and it would strike my bracket out.

**Location of counterpart**

The light of that night is a hundred and ninety-two years out. Where is it now? The form assumes the question has an answer. Attested: it has not. People moving differently disagree about which far-off events are happening at once, and none of them is wrong. Nearer home, a clock on the shelf a metre above my desk gains about three billionths of a second a year on one beside the stick. What all parties agree on is what happens when two things are in the same place at the same time. The world does not keep one clock. If it keeps anything, I should say it keeps the meetings; and I notice how well that suits my first bracket, which proves nothing.

Further out there is an edge. The expansion of space is speeding up, attested since 1998, and what drives it is unidentified, as is five-sixths of the matter. If the driver holds steady, which surveys since 2024 have put in some doubt, there is a distance, some sixteen billion light-years from any given reader, beyond which nothing that reader sends from today on will ever arrive; and the most that could ever be marked inside one such horizon is, in good company, about 10¹²² yes-or-noes.

Filled to that count, a horizon would be a record of nothing: every difference in it gone where nobody can read it. A stick cut everywhere is as blank as a stick cut nowhere. We live on the stretch where a notch can still be told from the wood.

**Counterpart**

*Counterpart: at large.*

Not *destroyed*: I cannot attest it, and neither can anyone. Not *lost*, since lost supposes a place it ought to have been. The other half went up a flue in 1834 and, if the rule holds, has been getting harder to read ever since. It is in no one's keeping, and I do not offer that as comfort.

You are the next hand on the slip. By your date you may know what I cannot: whether the two small weights took the grain, whether the driver held. If my brackets are wrong, do not rub them out. Write beside them.

The notches have reached you: three thumbs, a little finger, a row of barleycorns. Those copy. The grain does not. [Here I leave the object. I should like it to follow that what I could not say to you is the room you are reading this in. It does not follow. Here I return to it.]

You have the foil.

**Note on sources**

*Attested* means proved, or tested and not yet failed. It is whatever the entry states flat, and the edge only if the driver is constant.

*Calculated, never measured*: what a black hole can hold; that it evaporates.

*Probable*: that copying is why a stone lies in one place for everyone; the quantum unrest behind the ripples; the even beginning and the short furnace as the reason records run one way; the *stock* in *stockholder*.

*In good company*: surface, not volume, for any region properly marked out; the record returning from a black hole; boundary descriptions, and space built of grain, in paper universes only; that gravity can give grain to two small weights; the count inside the edge.

*Not known*: when a cut becomes final, or whether; how the penny was cut; why the beginning was unspent; what the driver is and whether it holds; what most of the matter is.

*Supplied*: [a fact is a fit, and the laws are its terms] is nearer a reading than a theory. I know of nothing that would damage it short of two records that would not fit. [The grain is the room] carries a good-company guess out of the paper universes and into this one.

*Blank*: Maker; What it is to hold it.

*Also supplied*, and outside brackets only because the entry could not be written without them: the cataloguer, the predecessor, the slip, this particular stock, the sayings, and the last line.

## How it was made, and what to trust

- **Where the idea came from:** Fable's own first draft was an essay about a world that makes one promise, that any two records, brought together, agree. A panel of four independent designs produced the tally stick, which says the same thing with an object. Both judges ranked it first and Fable rewrote on it.
- **What was checked:** the story went through fact-checkers for quantum physics, cosmology and the medieval history, an auditor of the known-versus-invented line, a cold reader and an editor. They found no outright errors of physics, but several corrections that mattered, and those are applied.
- **What was not re-checked:** the last edits were Fable's. Fable applied the checkers' wording and restored, in shorter form, some science the editor wanted cut (Noether, the sunlight arithmetic, the clock), since Kelly asked for the math and physics. That is why it runs about 2,950 words against the editor's 2,500.
- **Soft spots:** the two late-2026 status claims (the gravity experiment not yet done, dark energy's constancy in doubt) rest on knowledge to mid-2026 plus the checkers' web searches. A thirteenth-century foil surviving to be burned in 1834 is historically unlikely, which the text hedges.
