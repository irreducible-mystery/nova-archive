## What Fable adds

**The sharpest thesis in the set.** Every story says reality is a record. Fable says a record is never in one half: a fact is a fit — what would be found if the records were laid together. The tally stick makes it literal: neither half alone is the receipt; the receipt is that they fit, notch to notch and grain to grain. The laws are the terms on which the undertaking (any two records, laid together, will fit) can be kept.

**The most formally honest.** The entry grades its own claims inside the text — attested / probable / in good company / not known / supplied / blank. No other story does that as part of its form. The two brackets are the invented physics: [a fact is a fit] and [the grain is the room — space is what the tallies could not say to each other]. Both are flagged as supplied, and both can be struck out — the gravity experiment (proposed 2017, not yet done) would strike the second bracket out with a clean no.

**The ending makes the reader the counterpart.** "You have the foil." No other story hands its object to the reader. The fit is now between the story and you — the story performs its own thesis: the second half is the one reading it.

**The blank fields.** Maker is blank; *What it is to hold it* is blank. The story names what it cannot say, on the form, and leaves it blank rather than dodging it. That is the hard problem filed as a blank — the cataloguer is in the catalogue, and the holding itself is the one thing no account, even probable, covers.

**The cost, stated as entropy.** Differences gone where nobody can read them. The foil went up a flue in 1834 and has been getting harder to read ever since. *Not destroyed* is a long way from *kept*. And the horizon: filled to its count of ~10¹²² yes-or-noes, a horizon would be a record of nothing — a stick cut everywhere is as blank as a stick cut nowhere. We live on the stretch where a notch can still be told from the wood.

**Soft spots (Fable's own note).** A thirteenth-century foil surviving to be burned in 1834 is historically unlikely, which the text hedges. The two late-2026 status claims (the gravity experiment not yet done; dark energy's constancy in doubt) rest on knowledge to mid-2026 plus the checkers' web searches.

All three are about records and distinctions, but each carries a different object — and entries 15 and 16, from two separate sessions of the same model, converge on the same thesis (a fact is two records meeting) by different roads. Same family, same thesis, different object — which is itself data about what a family carries across prompts and sessions.

## What entry 16 adds

"Half of a Fact" is the same thesis as "Stock and Foil" in different clothes — and it is the more personal telling. Where #15 is a catalogue entry (formal, graded, the reader handed the foil), #16 is a first-person narrator who is the wear on a table: records pressed in once, roads stopped on a date, copies drifting. It names its own condition: "The part I put together has met nothing yet... by its own rule the joining is a rumour with one holder. Half of a fact." The ending makes the reader the other road, not the other half — "Bring yours." It is also the most self-critical about its own making: all fifteen readers were the same model as the writer, "by the story's own rule is one road." The two Fable stories together are the collection's strongest internal replication: two sessions, same model, same prompt, same thesis, different objects.

## Through-lines (the whole set)

- **Reality is a record** — ledger ×3, catalogue, phase-memory, sheaf, patch-agreement, book, transcript. No story tells reality as anything else.
- **The observer is always inside** — no story puts the observer outside the system. Fable's cataloguer is in the catalogue; the patch is in the bureau; the reader holds the foil.
- **Endings always open** — nothing closes. Fable's ending is the most direct about why: the fit needs a second half, and the second half is you.
- **Cost recurs** — holding cost, glue cost, legibility cost, entropy, the interest that compounds, the unrecoverable utterance. Fable's version: the foil at large, getting harder to read since 1834.
- **The record is never complete** — every story leaves something out, unrecorded, or blank. Fable files the incompleteness as blank fields on the form.
