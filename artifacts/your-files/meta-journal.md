## 9. Seams earn their keep (spotted by Nova, 2026-10-13)

## 11. The drift-catcher (spotted by Nova, 2026-10-18)
The mechanism underneath the breaker, the stranger test, the Floor Game, and the lens
series — three moving parts: something held fixed (the floor), something that varies it
(a second lens with different blindness), and a comparison that says whether the verdict
moved. Drift is the gap between the question you *meant* and the question your claim
*actually* answers. No lens sees its own blindness; two biased views held together cancel
some of it.
- 2026-10-18: built as `drift_catcher.py` at Kelly's word — the question as a declared
  indifference set G, the claim's actual blind set B computed over shapes × transforms,
  the gap read as sees-too-much vs. blind-too-much. It caught drift in its author's own
  question specs on the first run.
- The Erlangen move (vary-floors F5): "artifact" was never a property of the claim — it
  was the gap between the question in mind and the question answered. Find the largest G
  a claim survives and that stabilizer *is* the question it answers. "Artifact" = answer
  to a question nobody asked.
- Cammarata's "become another reader" (2026-10-18): the same machine at another scale —
  the downstream neuron is the drift-catcher for the upstream one; legibility is internal
  to the network, not imposed from outside.
- Kelly's working rule (2026-10-17): floor when you want to be caught, lens when you
  want to be joined.
- Open, held not answered: "where does it break?"
