  claim: topmost point  |  question: is it round?
    -> DRIFT: sees too much
       sees too much: ['mirror', 'reverse', 'rotate', 'scale', 'shuffle', 'translate']  (narrower question than asked)
    The classic. You asked a shape question; the claim answers a gravity one.

  claim: roundness  |  question: is it round?
    -> ANSWERS THE QUESTION
    The claim the series meant.

  claim: roundness  |  question: is it round (affine)?
    -> DRIFT: sees too much
       sees too much: ['shear']  (narrower question than asked)
    Same English word, different G: a sheared circle. W3's lesson, caught.

  claim: handedness (list)  |  question: is it drawn CCW?
    -> DRIFT: blind to too much
       blind too much: ['shear']  (broader question, or nothing)
    Order is data here: reverse/shuffle are correctly OUTSIDE G.

  claim: handedness (set)  |  question: is it drawn CCW?
    -> DRIFT: sees too much
       sees too much: ['translate']  (narrower question than asked)
    The set version smuggles order back in via the farthest pair.

  claim: vacuous  |  question: how many?
    -> ANSWERS THE QUESTION
    Blind to everything: passes every G, answers nothing.

  claim: raw coordinates  |  question: is it round?
    -> DRIFT: sees too much
       sees too much: ['mirror', 'reverse', 'rotate', 'scale', 'shuffle', 'translate']  (narrower question than asked)
    Blind to nothing: the other failure mode. Sees everything, answers nothing.

  claim: topmost index  |  question: which point is highest?
    -> DRIFT: blind to too much
       blind too much: ['mirror', 'shear']  (broader question, or nothing)
    Note what G leaves out: is list order data, or is the physical point?

============================================================================
WHAT GOT BUILT, AND WHAT IT STANDS ON
============================================================================
  The drift-catcher is F5's 'the question fixes G' turned into a tool:
  state the question as a set, compute the claim's blind set, read
  the gap. It does not certify truth. It locates the place where the
  question you meant and the question your claim answers come apart.

  Floors this script knows it stands on and is not varying:
    - the QUESTIONS dict: English turned into sets by fiat. W3's gap
      (word vs group) lives here, stated, not solved.
    - 'same verdict' means string equality (the unexamined reader).
    - five shapes, seven transforms: a sample, not the space (F1).
    - the worlds are still fixed. E2's criticism applies to this
      script exactly as it applied to the first one.
    - B == G never happens; 'ANSWERS THE QUESTION' is approximate,
      graded by the size of the gap, not boolean (E6).

  Use: when a claim and a question part ways, run this before arguing
  about the claim. Argue about the gap instead.
