## What the exam is

- **Credential:** HRCI Professional in Human Resources – California (PHRca). Stand-alone credential — you do **not** need to hold the PHR first, and holding the PHR shortens or exempts nothing.
- **What it tests:** applying California employment law in everyday HR work (not federal law — that's the PHR).
- **Format:** 115 questions total — 90 scored, mostly multiple-choice, + 25 unscored pretest items. 135 minutes + 30 minutes administration time. Pass mark 500 on HRCI's 100–700 scale.
- **Where/when:** year-round, at Pearson VUE test centres or via OnVUE remote proctoring. There is no fixed exam date — you pick a date when you apply, so the target date is yours to set.
- **Cost:** $100 application fee (non-refundable) + $395 exam fee = **$495 total** for one attempt. Fees do not cover study materials, retests, or transfers.
- **Validity:** 3 years. Recertification is California-specific credits every 3 years (one HRCI comparison lists 45 California-specific credits; another source lists 60 total with 15 California — verify on HRCI before planning) or retake the exam.
- **Pass rate:** HRCI reports **47% for the PHRca** (as of Dec 31, 2025) — the lowest of its certifications (aPHR 71%, PHR 72%, SPHR 76%). State-law content is the hard part; plan study time accordingly.

## Eligibility — you need only one route

1. Master's degree or higher + at least 1 year in a professional-level HR role, **or**
2. Bachelor's degree + at least 2 years in a professional-level HR role, **or**
3. At least 4 years in a professional-level HR role, with or without a degree.

No residence or employer-location requirement. Applications are accepted year-round.

## The five domains (HRCI content outline weightings)

| Domain | Weight | ~share of 90 scored Qs |
|---|---|---|
| Compliance and Risk Management | 29% | ~26 |
| Employment Lifecycle and Employee Relations | 26% | ~23 |
| Compensation / Wage and Hour | 21% | ~19 |
| Leaves of Absence and Benefits | 14% | ~13 |
| Health, Safety, and Workers' Compensation | 10% | ~9 |

One rule shapes how to study: **candidates are responsible for knowing the laws in effect in January of the calendar year in which they sit the exam** (HRCI outline). California passes new employment legislation most years, so study materials must be current-year, not a 2022 guide.
