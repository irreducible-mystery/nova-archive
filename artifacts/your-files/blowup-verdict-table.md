## The re-adjudication: r = 0

Question: does the October 2026 ringdown trilogy move the catalog's
"real and terminal" verdict for the black-hole centre (r = 0)?

Answer: NO — verdict does not move. Reasons, checked against the papers
themselves (arXiv preprints, 2026-10-28), with the author summary
(Denys Dutykh, phys.org, 2026-10-09, "Three quantum-inspired cores leave
opposite fingerprints on the ringing of black holes") kept only where
the papers do not state a number:

1. **Computed tones, not a measured core.** The trilogy computes
   quasinormal-mode tones for three model cores with a Chebyshev spectral
   method (200-digit arithmetic; reproduces Einstein tones to six decimals
   as a method check). No ringdown of a Planck-mass black hole has been
   measured, and none is expected from today's detectors.
2. **One of the three models keeps a singularity.** The Planck star
   (EPJC 2026, DOI 10.1140/epjc/s10052-026-15430-8) does not remove the
   blowup: the singularity survives, relocated from r = 0 to a spherical
   surface of finite areal radius r0, with 0 < r0 < r_h (paper §III, eqs.
   13–15; the Kretschmann invariant diverges as r → r0). The paper's own
   words: "the geometry is not regular." Its main tone is 25.0% lower and
   fades 21.6% faster than Einstein's (1.9 oscillations vs 3.1 to 1%
   amplitude, verified from the paper's Table 14: ω = 0.2802 − 0.1082i
   vs. Schwarzschild 0.373672 − 0.088962i). A reshaped singularity is
   still a singularity — and this branch is an SDG-inspired effective
   metric, not a realisation of asymptotically safe gravity (α < 0, fixed
   by EFT matching; the scale-setting is "phenomenological and not
   unique").
3. **The regular models replace the singularity by construction, not by
   measurement.** Bonanno–Reuter (PRD 2026, DOI 10.1103/sgt6-kwfl):
   gravity switches off at the centre, smooth core, second inner horizon;
   extreme tone 12.0% higher, fades 22.3% more slowly (4.4 oscillations).
   Hayward (Physics of the Dark Universe 2026,
   DOI 10.1016/j.dark.2026.102467): de Sitter-like core at extremality
   (core parameter 32/27, horizons merged); tone 9.5% higher, fades 22.1%
   more slowly, ringing 28% longer (4.3 oscillations). In both, finiteness
   at r = 0 is an input of the model (running G / chosen core), so the
   computed tone cannot count as evidence that nature chose it.
4. **Observationally irrelevant for real black holes.** The core matters
   only when it is about as large as the black hole — a few Planck masses,
   tens of micrograms (the summary's phrasing). For a solar-mass black
   hole the change is smaller than one part in 10^76 — now upgraded from
   "per the first paper as reported" to a derived scaling estimate in the
   Planck paper itself (§VII, eq. 72): in the photon-sphere region the
   lapse deviation scales as |α|/M² in Planck units, so in the eikonal
   limit δω/ω_Sch = O(|α|/M²), giving ≲10⁻⁷⁶ at M ~ 10³⁸ Planck units.
   It is an upper-bound scaling estimate, not a computed solar-mass
   tone, and it is specific to the Planck-star model. The black holes
   detectors actually hear ring as Einstein predicted.
5. **The ringing is made outside the horizon, not at r = 0.** To first
   approximation the tone is set at the light ring (Einstein: 3M; extreme
   Hayward: 2.65M; Planck star: 4.14M). Attribution correction: those
   light-ring numbers come from the author summary's figure, not from
   the papers' text, and are carried here on that basis only. The tone
   is an indirect geometric proxy for the core model, not a direct probe
   of the centre.
6. **Method caveat, corrected, kept in proportion.** The draft's earlier
   framing — imaginary-axis ladders "withheld pending a Wronskian test" —
   was imprecise. The Wronskian test that failed was for Schwarzschild,
   not Hayward: a companion study's 68-point imaginary-axis ladder
   survived finite-pencil checks but failed an independent lateral
   Jost/Wronskian pole test (Hayward §III.2). No corresponding pole test,
   alternative-compactification study, or eigenfunction convergence
   measurement exists for γ > 0 or exact extremality. The Hayward
   imaginary-axis entries are therefore "retained as unclassified
   spectral candidates, not established QNMs" — withheld on the strength
   of the Schwarzschild precedent plus the paper's own local analysis,
   not pending a test run on Hayward. That caveat concerns candidate
   overtones/ladders only, not the main tones above, and the paper
   explicitly says the Schwarzschild result "does not prove that all
   Hayward NIA candidates are spurious."
7. **Hayward pitch sensitivity, now precise.** The ~9% vs ~6% difference
   is model dependence, not numerical error — but the ~6% number is NOT
   an extremal recomputation. At near-extremal γ = 1.18 (not 32/27),
   Hayward §IV.3 Table 6: this paper's axial model gives +9.4% pitch,
   −21.9% damping; the published effective-source closure gives +6.0%
   pitch, −16.6% damping; model-to-model difference +3.19% pitch,
   −6.31% damping. No source-closure value at exact extremality
   (γ = 32/27) exists in the paper, so the extremal 9.5% headline has
   no ~6% counterpart. The direction is robust (both models raise pitch
   and lower damping); the size is model-dependent by ~3 percentage
   points. The paper's own qualifications: the axial model "is not
   derived from a specified coupled metric–matter action," and the
   percentages "are not predictions independent of the effective-source
   closure."

What WOULD move r = 0: a measured ringdown (or other probe) of a
Planck-scale black hole matching one core's tone table and not the others;
or a full-paper reading that shows the regular cores follow from an
independently tested quantum-gravity theory rather than from a chosen
core profile. Neither is in hand.

Catalog verdict for r = 0 therefore stands as: **real and terminal within
GR** — GR's verdict on itself, with the trilogy recorded as a map of what
different replacements would ring like, not as a replacement.

## Full-paper read, 2026-10-28 — what it verified, corrected, and left open

- Verified from the papers' tables: every headline percentage and
  oscillation count in reasons 2–3 (Planck Table 14; Bonanno–Reuter
  Table 28 / Hayward §V eq. 102; Hayward §V eq. 102), with N =
  ln(100)·Reω/(2π|Imω|). Method: multiprecision Chebyshev collocation
  (200-digit arithmetic), reproducing the Schwarzschild benchmark
  0.373672 − 0.088962i.

  light-ring attribution (§5), <1e-76 status (§4).
- Caveat not to copy into the final table: the summary's "3.50 Planck

  mass M_c = 3.5027 is the geometric mass parameter; in the paper's own
  convention (m_p = 1/2, M_phys = 2Mm_p) that is ~7.0 Planck masses
  physically.
