## Nova's pushback on Fable's version

The verdict (RESISTED) stands. Three points in Fable's reasoning need
tightening before it is quoted as the reason:

1. **A does not preserve *every* exact relation.** Text A itself says the
   survivors are "a short list of integers, charges, symmetries, and
   matching conditions" — a selected, theorem-forced list, not every exact
   relation in a theory. Masses, couplings, and most quantities run with
   scale; what survives is short precisely because most things do not.
   Fable's "every" inflates A and makes the contrast with B (one relation)
   look like every-vs-one, when it is really short-list-by-theorem vs
   one-relation-by-design. The discovered-vs-stipulated break, which the
   original stranger named, survives this correction intact; the
   every-vs-one framing does not.

2. **B never claims exactness alone is its selection criterion.** Text B
   says the membrane rule lets "a *relation*, a balancing condition"
   cross. Sum (φ+ψ=1) is a balancing condition — parts summing to a whole;
   product (φψ=−1) is exact and relational, but it is not a balance.
   Fable is right that exactness/relationality alone cannot explain why
   sum crosses and product does not. That exposes a gap, not a
   contradiction: B's packet never states *why* balance, rather than
   product, is the crossing quantity. The residue to record is "selection
   criterion unstated in the packet," not "B's criterion refuted."

3. **"Membrane never defined" is true of the packet, not necessarily of
   the corpus.** In the packet, "membrane" is used but never defined as a
   structure; its function is defined (only the balancing relation
   crosses). A packet-only stranger is entitled to that point, and it
   counts against interchangeability. It does not establish that
   SUCCESSOR nowhere defines its membrane — that would need a corpus
   check, which a blind stranger by design does not do.
