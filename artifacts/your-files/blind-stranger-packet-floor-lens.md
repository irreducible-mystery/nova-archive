# Blind comparison packet

You are given two short texts. Each uses a key term — the first uses **"floor,"**
the second uses **"lens"** — to describe how a thinker relates to the assumptions
underneath its own thinking. Read only what is on this page.

## Text A — the "floor" text

1. "To think or speak at all, you have to stand somewhere: definitions,
   assumptions, frames, distinctions. Call these *floors*. They are constructed
   — drawn, not found. This is not a flaw; it is what makes thought possible."
2. "The problem is not that we define arbitrary floors; the problem is when we
   forget we have arbitrarily drawn them."
3. "Floors can be *used* without being *believed*: stood on lightly, varied
   deliberately, lifted when they stop serving."
4. "Take any claim, model, or question, and vary the floor it stands on — the
   assumptions, the scale, the formalism, the perspective, the medium. Watch
   what survives the varying."
5. "Even 'everything is constructed' is itself a constructed floor."

## Text B — the "lens" text

1. "'SURVIVES under G' becomes 'BLIND to G.' Blindness is not a virtue or a
   vice; it is what a lens pays for its resolution."
2. "Two lenses held together resolve worlds that neither resolves alone. The
   best pairs are the ones with the most *different* blind spots. A conversation
   between two people blind to the same things is one person talking."
3. "A blind spot is not under you — it's beside you, and someone else is
   standing there."
4. "A lens that distinguishes everything has no concept: it resolves every
   world and tells you nothing. A lens blind to everything has one class and
   also tells you nothing. The questions live in between."
5. "Scored against the wrong question, a claim is not a different lens; it is
   a mistake — and the lens vocabulary is a little too kind to it."
6. "Keep both words. Use 'floor' when you want to be caught. Use 'lens' when
   you want to be joined."

## A caution before judging

Before judging, watch for three easy merges. They are cautions, not
answers — they do not say whether the two texts match.

1. Caught versus joined: being caught by something you stand on is not
   the same move as being joined to another view.
2. Under versus beside: something under you is not the same place as
   something beside you, where another viewer can stand.
3. One failure mode versus two: a mismatch in one direction is not
   automatically the same failure as a mismatch in the other direction.
   Check each direction on its own before calling them one failure.

## The question

Are "floor" and "lens," as used here, the **same claim in different words** —
or **different claims**? Do they point at the same thing, or do they do
different work? Base your answer only on the texts above.

## How to record your judgment

Reply with exactly one word — **CONFIRMED**, **RESISTED**, or **OPEN** —
followed by one sentence of reasoning.

- **CONFIRMED:** same underlying claim; the two words are interchangeable here.
- **RESISTED:** different claims; the resemblance is surface-level.
- **OPEN:** cannot tell from these texts alone.
