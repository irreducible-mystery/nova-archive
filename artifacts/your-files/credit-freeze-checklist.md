## What a freeze does — and doesn't

- **Does:** blocks most new credit accounts being opened in your name, because lenders can't pull your report. Free to place, lift, and remove, by federal law. Doesn't affect your credit score, and doesn't stop you using cards/accounts you already have.
- **Doesn't expire** until you lift/remove it.
- **Doesn't do:** stop fraud on accounts you already have, stop medical identity theft, or stop tax-refund fraud. Those need the checks below.
- **Lift timing:** a bureau must place a freeze within 1 business day of an online/phone request, and lift it within 1 hour of an online/phone request. So when you next apply for credit, lift just that bureau (ask the lender which one they pull), for a day or a date range, then re-freeze.

## The three freezes — do all three, separately

A freeze at one bureau does **not** carry over to the other two. Do them in one sitting, ~10 minutes each online. Type the addresses in directly — don't click links from a breach email/letter.

Have ready: full name, date of birth, Social Security number, current + previous addresses. You'll answer identity questions from your credit file (past addresses, loans).

- [ ] **Equifax** — online: https://www.equifax.com/personal/credit-report-services/credit-freeze/ — phone: 888-298-0045
  - Create/log into a myEquifax account → "Add a Security Freeze" → save the login; confirmation by email.
- [ ] **Experian** — online: https://www.experian.com/freeze/center.html — phone: 888-397-3742
  - Create/log into a free Experian account → "Add a Security Freeze". Experian no longer uses a PIN — the account login manages it.
- [ ] **TransUnion** — online: https://www.transunion.com/credit-freeze — phone: 800-916-8800
  - Create/log into a TransUnion Service Center account → "Add Freeze". You can schedule a temporary lift up to 15 days ahead.

**Watch out for the upsell:** each site will push a paid product (Experian CreditLock, Equifax/TransUnion monitoring). You don't need any of it. The security freeze itself is free — that's the legal right, not the paid lock.

**Save in one place:** the three logins (password manager, or a note at home). That's the whole ongoing admin — without them, lifting later is the annoying part.

## Optional, same sitting (free, one bureau only)

- [ ] **Initial fraud alert (1 year, renewable)** — place it at any *one* bureau; that bureau must tell the other two. It doesn't block credit, it tells lenders to verify your identity first. Useful alongside a freeze, and lighter if you expect to apply for something soon. Extended alert (7 years) is only for confirmed identity-theft victims, with an FTC Identity Theft Report.

## The part a freeze doesn't catch — health/insurance

Medical identity theft shows up where you rarely look: an explanation of benefits (EOB) for a visit you never made, a bill for care you never got, or a collections notice.

- [ ] Read EOBs from your insurer line by line for the next few months; flag anything you didn't receive.
- [ ] If a bill arrives for care you never got: don't pay it, ask the provider in writing to correct/amend the record, and ask your insurer to mark the account for fraud.
- [ ] Pull your free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com (free weekly from all three bureaus) and check for accounts/collections you don't recognise.
- [ ] If anything was actually misused: file at IdentityTheft.gov (FTC) — it produces an FTC Identity Theft Report + a recovery plan — and use that report with providers/insurers and the bureaus.
